Welcome!

So, you’re looking to start your music making journey… great! It’s much simpler and less intimidating than most people think. All you have to do is set up a music program and some tools, optionally with at least some decent gear.

I'll guide you through how to set up a DAW (Digital Audio Workstation), some basic tools, a few beginner concepts, and a demo of what it might look like to make a song.

Quick checklist

  • A laptop or computer
  • Headphones or speakers
  • A free DAW (we’ll use Cakewalk)
  • One free instrument pack

Programs and Tools

Mentioned previously, you’ll need a DAW, for this guide we’ll be using a completely free DAW called Cakewalk by Bandlab. Additionally, unless you have instruments of your own to record and a recording setup (too much work!), you’ll also need plugins and digital instruments, not to worry though! you won’t have to pay a dime. Obviously you can if you want, there are plenty of really good paid plugins out there, but for this guide all software we use will be completely free. You also might find some basic music theory useful, for this guide specifically we’ll be making something akin to jazz fusion or video game music, but feel free to experiment with your sound, it’s YOUR sound after all!

Other Items

For hardware you’ll want to have a laptop or a computer that can atleast handle music production software, and some way to listen to the audio you’re making, preferrably a studio quality headset. This however isn’t strictly necessary, you could technically still make music on even crappy speakers, but! It’ll be a very useful investment, not only for making music, but even for just listening to things (the audio quality is obviously better!) For this guide you don’t need anything else, although if you have a MIDI controller or something like that, good on you!

Mindset and Inspiration

It’s great that you’re starting your journey as an artist! But please, try not to be too harsh on yourself, have fun with your projects! They’re not assignments that have deadlines (well unless it’s commissioned), so don’t stress on the quality and stuff too much, just have fun with it, Additionally, picking up an instrument is not only fun but it’ll also increase your understanding of music, so feel free to try a guitar or a piano or whatever!
